# Founder Growth TODO

## First offer

Launch Naelao's first managed growth service for solo founders and very small teams with an active
product, real customers, and a recurring growth task they still handle by hand. Start with
founder-led B2B SaaS as a useful screening hypothesis. Compare it with the small-business template
path on repeated demand, willingness to pay for setup, time to first useful result, and how much
work carries over to the next customer. Keep the first offer specific enough to quote and deliver.

The offer is to help founders get more customers from demand they already have. Start discovery
with warm inbound follow-up, trial activation and onboarding, stalled opportunities, and customer
reactivation. Pick one workflow after multiple independent founders describe the same problem and
at least one is willing to pay for a defined pilot. Leave the other workflows available as
hypotheses. The first candidate offer is:

> We run your inbound follow-up. Connect your inbox and lead source, tell us what makes someone a
> good fit, and we'll set up a workflow to qualify and follow up with those leads. You'll have one
> place to review exceptions and see who replied or booked.

The managed service can eventually become a self-serve install that produces the same result.
This follows the idea of selling completed work described in
[Sequoia's Services: The New Software](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software).

## Find and qualify founders

| Source | Signals to capture |
| --- | --- |
| Product Hunt and other launch platforms | Launch interest, early signups and what happened after launch week. Look for gaps in activation or follow-up. |
| Reddit | A founder describes a recurring bottleneck in their own words. Look for independent repeats across relevant founder or SaaS communities. |
| Discord | Join relevant founder communities, follow local rules and use conversations members make available for this purpose. Ask a specific question when an introduction or reply is welcome. |
| Indie Hackers and public build-in-public posts on X and elsewhere | Milestones, experiments, active customers, inbound volume and recurring growth work the founder keeps putting off. |
| Starter Story, YouTube, podcasts and newsletters | How customers find the product, what happens after a feature or traffic spike, and any concrete operational problem the founder mentions. |

Keep one private prospect sheet with business/product, founder or public handle, channel, source URL
and date, evidence excerpt or accurate paraphrase, traction signal, suspected workflow, confidence,
permitted contact route, contact status and next step. Deduplicate by founder and business across
channels. Distinguish a founder's own statement from a hypothesis based on public activity. Use
recent evidence and known business contact paths. Keep private community details within their
allowed context and verify contact information instead of guessing it.

Prioritize repeatable problems across founders over long lists of weak leads. In discovery,
ask how often the task happens, how they do it today, what is missed, what that costs, which tools
hold the data, and whether they would pay to hand it off. Group answers by workflow. Look for
shared inputs, decisions, exceptions and success measures. Start with the channel where each
founder discussed the problem when they welcome replies. Offer WhatsApp as an optional place to
continue by text after they express interest.

> Hey [Name], I saw you mention [specific detail] in [launch, post, or interview]. What happens to
> [those trial users, demo requests, or past customers] today? I'm putting together a service that
> runs one recurring growth job for founder-led businesses. If this is a bottleneck for you, happy
> to compare notes here or over WhatsApp.

## Pilot and commercial terms

Quote a prepaid setup fee for a defined pilot, covering workflow mapping, connections, rules and
initial setup in the founder's Space. Agree on a monthly retainer for operating the workflow,
handling exceptions, making agreed adjustments and reporting. Scope the pilot around one
workflow, a start and review date, an agreed customer segment, and a clear definition of done.
Price setup and operation so the service is viable in a month with zero commission.

Consider commission for the ongoing service once both sides can attribute results from a
shared record. Agree in writing on the eligible cohort, pre-existing opportunities and other
exclusions, the attribution window, the cash or margin basis, how refunds are handled, the
percentage, and the settlement and dispute process. Use an auditable source such as linked
CRM and payment records. A first candidate cohort is qualified, previously stalled leads that
receive Naelao follow-up and later become paying customers. An illustrative agreement of 10%
of the first 90 days of cash collected from those deals after refunds would pay $500 on $5,000
collected. Set actual amounts with the founder.

Measure delivery directly. For inbound follow-up, capture the baseline and track the share of
qualified inquiries followed up within the agreed window, as well as exceptions and founder time
spent. Track replies, bookings and cash collected as downstream results. Report on the agreed
cohort and describe what can be attributed from the available records. Keep guaranteed
deliverables tied to work Naelao controls.

## Where the work lives

Each client owns a Naelao Space, their provider connections, and their operating records. Use a
Project in that Space for the pilot workflow and its dashboard. Give the service explicitly scoped
access for setup, operation and exception handling. The founder can see activity, review drafts
and exceptions, and revoke access or take over the workflow. Verify actual permission and handoff
support during implementation. Use the agency's own Space for prospecting, pilot coordination,
and reusable playbooks. A Project there can track the relationship using minimal client references.
Evaluate client subspaces or a multi-client agency console once several operators have a recurring
need to manage multiple client Spaces.

Start as a managed service. An operator configures the first workflow, reviews early actions with
the founder, and handles exceptions. Automate predictable cases within approved rules and measure
how much operator time remains. The agency factory is the ability to install the same service for
another founder by changing inputs, qualification rules, voice, permissions and approval settings.
Give customers a simple outcome dashboard and consider self-service once setup and daily operation
are predictable. Reuse existing Space, Project, template and AgentPort permission surfaces when
they support the workflow.
