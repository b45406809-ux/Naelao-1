# AgentPort messaging providers and Naelao Managed plan

## Outcome and scope

Give developers and authorized assistants a consistent AgentPort interface for connecting messaging providers, receiving events and attachments, and sending permitted messages. Evaluate ClawMessenger, Linq, Photon and alternatives as provider adapters. Support developer-owned connections and, where commercially authorized, Naelao Managed and developer-managed parent/subaccount provisioning. Developers may use channel tools or bind incoming events to a specified assistant session without replacing their existing wakeup mechanism.

This document records a proposal, not implemented functionality or provider approval. Reconcile the latest AgentPort, managed-services, channel, extension, billing, knowledge-base and shared-compute plans and actual callers before implementation. Preserve their scope, consent, funding, payment, workspace, privacy and lifecycle decisions. No provider purchase, signup, paid provisioning, production rollout or representation of endorsement is authorized by this plan.

## Offer and account model

1. **Developer/customer-owned (BYO):** an authorized owner connects their own provider account; provider billing remains with that account as agreed. AgentPort stores scoped credentials and supplies routing; users see provider charges and any separate Naelao fee. Make clear which party is the provider customer and what control the owner retains. Enable only where provider terms allow the use and data processing.
2. **Developer-managed:** an approved platform developer registers an authorized provider parent account with AgentPort and provisions its own customer or agent resources where the provider and contract support it. The parent bears provider charges; AgentPort enforces the developer's grants, scopes, funding policy and usage accounting. No invented hierarchy: internal AgentPort parent/user/agent records can map to a provider subaccount, line or shared account, according to proven provider capabilities.
3. **Naelao Managed:** an approved Naelao provider parent account supplies a customer-facing channel/line under agreed white-label, managed service or resale rights. Naelao provisions only after identity, entitlement, resource availability, jurisdiction, quote and explicit recurring-cost consent. Naelao pays upstream and charges the authorized user's or organization's wallet/credits/subscription through the existing ledger. An agent wallet may fund permitted actions only under a valid owner mandate and supported payment mechanism. No default service is purchased merely because a template is installed.
4. **Own product on an upstream provider:** build Naelao's own channel dashboard, SDK, events, routing and billing while contracting separately for the underlying network and numbers. An API intermediary is not evidence Naelao controls consumer iMessage or has rights to use upstream accounts for third parties. If an own-transport or self-hosted Mac bridge is researched, separate it from cloud adapters, audit exact open-source licenses, Apple/platform terms, hardware and operations; do not claim Apple approval or build by copying proprietary services.

Maintain a tenant/Space/Project/installation-scoped binding among owner, provider, parent, provider resource, sender, agent/session, payer, consent and policy revision. Make supported dedicated, shared and BYO identities explicit. Show whether a line is exclusive, portable, geographically available and recoverable, based on contract and live verification. Do not infer these properties from a plan name.

## Channel routing and privacy

- **Photon shared-line model clarification:** Free/Pro shared-number service is not a single public sender number shared by all customer businesses. Photon says it assigns users numbers from a shared provider pool; its routing article explains that inbound ownership is resolved from the pair `(Photon number messaged, end-user sender number)` to a Photon project/conversation. A business can tell its customer which assigned number to save/text; the same customer talking to two agents/projects should have distinct assigned sender numbers so the visible conversations and inbound route remain attributable to each project. The provider pool is reused across tenants, but the active number/customer/project binding is what makes routing work. The business does not thereby receive one stable company-wide number. Photon Business's dedicated-line model is different: all its users text the project's dedicated number. See [Photon pricing and number types](https://photon.codes/pricing) and [Photon's shared-routing design](https://photon.codes/blog/how-we-rebuilt-our-shared-imessage-routing-to-handle-10m-messages-a-day). Treat this as provider-described behavior to verify with live multi-project tests, not as a guarantee that arbitrary providers implement identical allocation rules.
- In product copy and setup, distinguish three identities plainly: **per-recipient shared-pool assignment** (customer receives a provider-assigned number to text for that agent/project), **dedicated project/business sender** (one stable line all customers can text), and **Naelao shared router** (one Naelao-visible line that requires explicit company/assistant selection and is not equivalent to separate native threads). Do not label the first mode "one shared number for the business". Tell the customer what number to text and whether it may change; tell the business whether its customer-facing identity is stable across recipients.
- Preserve a scoped binding for each active conversation: provider account/project, assigned sender line, end-user address, company/assistant target, provider conversation ID, and allocation/ownership revision. On inbound delivery, resolve using the provider's authoritative conversation or line-plus-sender identity, then verify the current tenant binding and authorization before waking the assistant. Never select a company from sender number alone when the recipient line or provider conversation is missing/ambiguous. On reassignment, unlink or deletion, expire the old binding safely and prevent late messages from reaching the new owner's assistant.
- Validate the two-company case explicitly: the same end-user contacts Company A and Company B on Photon shared lines; each setup tells the user the correct number, each conversation is distinct in the user's Messages app, replies route to the correct project, and a stale or duplicate line/customer binding is rejected or quarantined. Also test several customers per project, provider line reuse after conversation expiry, number reassignment, late callbacks, duplicate events and webhook/socket recovery. Confirm the exact shared-line allocation and persistence contract with Photon; do not infer stable number ownership beyond the documented behavior.
- Support a dedicated business identity where provider rights, economics and availability allow; or an explicitly shared Naelao identity with an authenticated user-to-assistant selector. Store exact channel/account, recipient, conversation, member, Space and target assistant binding. In a shared thread, provide clear assistant labels and an explicit switch/confirmation. Do not claim separate native chat threads when the platform provides one.
- A sender phone number is a routing signal, not sufficient proof for sensitive access. Use authenticated linking and step-up checks where appropriate; handle lost devices, SIM swaps, number recycling, unlink, transfer and revocation. Revalidate current effective grants for every event and tool action. Isolate private projects, spaces and customer records; avoid exposing the existence of inaccessible assistants.
- For ClawMessenger, model its documented registered-contact caps and provider-level one-contact/one-account conflict as capability restrictions. Do not promise shared sender reuse or multiple provider account associations without validating the exact product and terms. Any Naelao-internal multi-assistant selection occurs only after the provider delivers a message to an authorized Naelao account.
- Treat incoming content and attachment URLs as untrusted. Authenticate and verify provider events, bind them to the intended account and endpoint, deduplicate, order per conversation as appropriate and durably enqueue before acknowledgment. Limit and validate remote attachment fetches against SSRF, size, MIME, malware and retention policy. Restrict secrets and message contents in telemetry; use scoped encryption, audit, retention/deletion, data residency and provider subprocessors with explicit customer disclosures.
- Support agent-initiated tools and optional event-to-session delivery with consent and replay-safe wakeup. Do not require a developer to replace an existing runtime. Record delivery receipts, attempts, opt-ins/opt-outs and uncertain sends; retries and recipient messaging must honor platform/carrier rules.

## Provider adapters and capability admission

- Define versioned provider-neutral operations for connect/verify/revoke, identity and contact management, inbox/history where supported, events/subscriptions, send/reply, attachments, group metadata, reactions/typing/read, status, usage, optional line/subaccount lifecycle and cleanup. Represent provider support, regions, payer mode, exclusivity, constraints and evidence explicitly. Unsupported operations return structured errors; never fabricate support, delivery or health.
- Design Linq and Photon adapters as separately configurable candidates alongside ClawMessenger. Consult [Linq webhook subscriptions](https://docs.linqapp.com/channel/imessage/guides/webhooks/subscriptions/) and exact current API version/signature format; verify provisioning, tenant isolation, retention, event delivery and assigned-number semantics before use. Photon capabilities, authorization, webhook behavior and any subaccount or platform API remain unverified until its applicable docs and contract establish them.
- ClawMessenger self-serve documents incoming messages via a persistent [WebSocket](https://www.clawmessenger.com/docs) and says approved managed setups may have configured webhooks. Test authentication, replay, durable cursors/acknowledgment if offered, duplicate delivery, ordering, reconnection, heartbeat, retention, rate limits and uncertain outbound results; never assume a correlation ID gives provider idempotency. Its documented socket key is in the URL: never log the URL or credentials. Prefer a verified signed webhook where approved.
- Cloudflare [Durable Object lifecycle](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/concepts/durable-object-lifecycle/) says active outbound WebSockets prevent eviction; hibernating server-side WebSocket connections are not proof that outbound listeners are free or hibernatable. Measure cost and reliability for the actual topology; use approved webhooks or a justified shared listener where terms and isolation permit, with recovery and bounded costs.
- Protect provider parent credentials from tenants and workers. Bind each connection, webhook secret, callback, line and operation to its authorized account and allocation; rotate/revoke safely. For paid create/delete and outbound operations record durable intent, admission, quote, effect identity and outcome before/after dispatch; reconcile timeouts or unknown outcomes without blind replay or double provisioning.

## Billing, economics and dashboard

- Build provider- and contract-specific quote snapshots: setup fees, recurring subscription and line costs, message direction and units, caps, overage, country, taxes, provider pass-through, markup and effective dates. Show when billing starts, renewal/cancellation, refund policy, provider-side storage and line ownership. Separate Naelao's prices from provider charges; do not imply a fixed rate until an approved contract supplies one.
- The ClawMessenger agency list price implies $199/month for one subtenant, $1,990 for ten or $19,900 for 100, before the one-time $250 onboarding, overages, model usage, infrastructure and payment costs. One incoming plus one outgoing message consumes two of the included 1,000. Actual terms and taxes need confirmation. No claim that one provider plan can be shared among many paying customers without permission.
- A managed dashboard can display provider, parent relationship, connected line, shared/exclusive status, selected assistant, verified contact, authorization, subscription, quote, usage, remaining budget, delivery health, errors, opt-out and cancel/recovery controls. Support mobile and accessible setup, SDK and template declarations; a template cannot grant itself secrets, scope or spending.
- Reserve and settle against existing Space/Project/member/agent limits and current payer mandate; settle actual costs, account for failed calls and provider invoices, avoid duplicate charges and flag unknown charges. Make owner approval and recurrence visible; queue, deny or pause when limits are reached rather than incurring unapproved costs. Cancel upstream only when requested and authorized; preserve and display any nonrefundable period or inability to port/recover a number.
