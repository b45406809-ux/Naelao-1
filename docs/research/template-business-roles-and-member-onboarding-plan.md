# Template-defined business roles and member onboarding

## 1. Outcome and fixed requirements

Let template builders define business roles and onboarding guidance for a Space. An authorized Space owner/admin reviews and assigns those roles during invitation or later membership changes. Each role proposes the projects, microapps, knowledge, shared data, assistant behavior, permitted actions, spending limits and approvals appropriate to the business. The platform resolves those proposals to actual host-enforced grants; a role label, template instruction, AI-generated text or hidden UI element never constitutes authorization.

**Builder-defined roles are a fixed product requirement.** The template builder creates and names the business roles, their descriptions, onboarding instructions and proposed access bundles for each template; these are not limited to Naelao's built-in generic Space or Project roles. The pharmacy roles below are examples only and must not be hard-coded as the available choices. A template may define different roles for another industry or multiple roles for one business. The host validates the builder's definitions and maps approved bundles to enforceable permissions; an authorized customer owner/admin reviews and assigns roles, subject to applicable authority and Space policy. The builder's proposal does not independently grant customer access, and any customer customization or mandatory template rule must be explicit and disclosed.

When an assistant acts for a member, it may access only what that member is currently authorized to access, further restricted by assistant-specific policy and operation grants. Shared business records remain in their authorized shared Project or application and can be used by other permitted members. Private chats, user-scoped data and private projects are not silently shared. Autonomous workflows, scheduled jobs, customer channel handlers and provider events without an acting user require their own explicitly authorized service identity and narrowly scoped grants; never borrow an owner's authority merely because no user is present.

This is an implementation plan, not a statement of deployed or production-ready functionality. Integrate it into the ongoing managed-services, guided-template, shared-Space coordination, microapp, mature knowledge, extensible-resource and planner/worker missions. Reconcile current source and progress before changes; preserve current worktree and newer work. Do not deploy, enable rollout, send invitations or contact users/providers without the relevant existing authorization. Keep security-sensitive paths disabled until their acceptance gates pass.

## 2. Illustrative template and business policy

| Suggested role | Example shared access | Example constraints |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner | Administer the business and explicitly authorized Projects and resources. | Sensitive actions and genuinely private Projects follow the selected disclosed policy. |
| Accountant | Use the accounting Project, approved finance knowledge and accounting actions. | No website publishing, payroll, private clinical records or other resource without an explicit applicable grant. |
| Inventory staff | Use authorized stock tools, shared stock records and selected supplier information. | No payroll or private contracts unless granted. |
| Customer service | Use published customer answers and authorized channel operations. | No private finance or supplier contracts by default. |
| Website manager | Edit and, where separately permitted, publish the website Project. | No accounting access without a grant. |

These are illustrative software roles, not legal or professional pharmacy assignments. A real pharmacy template must be reviewed with operators and relevant professionals. Template defaults are suggestions, not a promise that one role fits every business or a license for clinical actions.

## 3. Versioned business-role contract

- Define a backward-compatible signed template contract for builder-authored business roles with stable role IDs, localized names/descriptions, recommended generic Space role, referenced template Project/extension/microapp/collection IDs, requested action-level grants, assistant policy/instructions, onboarding steps and spending/approval suggestions. Support arbitrary valid role definitions within documented bounds; built-in generic roles can be underlying permissions but must not limit the business roles a builder can define. Use portable symbolic references resolved to the specific installed Space resources; never embed another tenant's resource IDs, private data, credentials or active grants.
- Separate role purpose and assistant instructions from authorization. A template author may propose roles and least-privilege defaults; only an authorized owner/admin may approve actual grants within their own authority, organization policy, subscription and relevant provider contracts. Clearly flag privileged roles, public publishing, personal/financial/health-related data, paid actions and authority expansion. Omission or invalid/unknown rules fail closed; old blueprints remain functional without invented roles.
- Support multiple roles per member with deterministic grant composition, explicit restrictions/denies where the product supports them, no privilege escalation and a clear effective-permissions explanation. Explicitly specify precedence among template defaults, organization policy, Space policy, Project membership, resource ACL, individual exceptions, installation grants, assistant clamp and provider authorization. Do not assume an ordinary hierarchical rank covers all business responsibilities. Validate role combinations and duplicate/conflicting declarations.
- Resolve and record exact template version, resource versions, approval actor, role assignment, effective grants and policy revision. Template updates must preview role/permission/cost changes, require appropriate approval, use expected-version checks and avoid silently widening existing members' privileges. Handle resource renames, removed roles, project transfers, role deletion and rollback without orphaning or silently escalating users. Preserve owner-approved overrides and document whether they survive an upgrade.
- Provide an SDK and accessible visual template-builder UI to author roles, map project/microapp actions, select knowledge and assistant defaults, test sample member permissions, package them and preview onboarding. Export/import round trips must preserve role definitions without including real customer members or data.

## 4. Invitation and onboarding workflow

1. An authorized inviter selects the target Space, invitee, one or more template-defined or owner-configured roles, and a bounded invitation expiry. Reuse existing identity/invitation flows where they actually apply; do not conflate product end-user, organization, Space, Project and external customer identities or link accounts merely by email.
2. Resolve a preview of actual named resources and actions the invitee will receive, their assistant's maximum access, optional channels, costs/seat entitlement and any private-data disclosures. The inviter may choose permitted modifications; unknown, inaccessible, revoked or uninstalled resources remain unavailable. Require explicit approval for sensitive, public, admin and paid capabilities.
3. Accept an invitation through authenticated identity and a one-time, expiring, audience- and Space-bound token; prevent reuse, forgery, cross-tenant redemption and unauthorized forwarding. Check current inviter authority, Space status, policy/template revision, requested grants, seat limits and budget again at redemption. A changed proposal requires a fresh review where authority or cost expands.
4. Apply membership, role assignments, Project membership and resource grants through durable, idempotent, auditable operations. Track pending, applied, failed, compensated and uncertain states; resume after crashes and do not report successful onboarding while required grants are missing. Avoid partial privilege escalation: until required state and checks complete, fail closed for dependent access. Use existing transaction boundaries/outbox and reconcile cross-service work safely.
5. Give the member a welcome flow showing their role, allowed apps/Projects, available assistant, relevant instructions, assigned tasks and limits. Offer guided setup and accessibility/mobile support without exposing private resource names. Clearly distinguish a role-based dashboard from a private conversation and identify what actions require approval.

Do not send real invitations as part of implementation or testing without explicit authorization; use simulated delivery locally and disposable accounts for approved live tests.

## 5. Effective authorization and assistant inheritance

- Derive current effective authority server-side for human, assistant, delegated worker, extension, channel and API callers. Intersect the acting user's membership and current resource permissions with role assignments, assistant policy, signed installation grants, task scope, provider authority, budget and runtime capability. Apply minimum necessary access; ordinary assistants and subagents cannot use the Space owner's privileges on behalf of a less-privileged member.
- Extend and audit the existing Project assistant clamp for knowledge retrieval and citations, scoped microapp reads/writes, business actions, workspace files/history, connectors, secret use, model/tools, browser, channel messages/attachments, background jobs, custom resources, caches, exports and other active callers. Enforce in the host/mutation/read service, not solely in prompts or the frontend. Avoid implicit grant by simply knowing a resource ID or receiving an event. Use audience-filtered coordination facts as untrusted advisory context only.
- For no-user cron, event and provider wakes, create a named, owner-authorized service principal with exact scopes, lifecycle, allowed actions, source/channel/beneficiary, budget, approval and revocation policy. Record why it ran and which grants authorized it. Do not treat event payload identity, a template instruction or undefined user identity as owner access. A customer message does not inherit an employee's or owner's private permissions.
- Reconcile owner/admin private-Project authority across SpaceDO, Knowledge, routing, assistant and extension paths. Make organization policy explicit and user-visible, specify whether private Projects can exclude admins and how emergency/administrative recovery is audited, then enforce the same policy across every path. Until policy and enforcement are verified, do not promise universal owner visibility or absolute privacy.
- Support assistant-specific `inherit`, read-only and none semantics or finer action policies where verified, with `assistant <= acting principal` as an invariant. Disallow stale cached roles: recheck before retrieval, citation, side effects, publication and delivery; cancel/drain or deny in-flight work on revocation as applicable. Preserve private chat access separately from shared application-record permissions.

## 6. Shared records, lifecycle and privacy

- Place accounting records, inventory, approved reports and other company work in explicitly scoped shared app/Project storage, not a member's private chat or a personal runtime. Changes through UI, SDK or assistant use the same action API, authorization, expected-version checks and audit records. An authorized owner and other permitted members see current data without automatically reading the author's private conversation.
- Define permissions for record/table/action read, write, approval, export, delete and manage separately when business risk requires it. Support separation of duties where configured, e.g. a person who prepares a payment cannot necessarily approve it. Do not assume Project editor status authorizes every action, raw file, secret or provider effect.
- Provide clear member role changes, temporary grants, suspension, removal, re-invitation, ownership transfer and role/template version updates. Revoke direct and assistant/delegated access, provider sessions, subscriptions and relevant caches; stop or reassign in-flight work with reliable receipts and explicit unknown results. Preserve shared business records and authorized colleagues' access. Follow disclosed privacy, data retention, export, backup and legal-hold rules; avoid automatic deletion of shared data when a staff member leaves.
- Integrate shared-Space blackboard and coordination policies: only permitted members see approved facts; discovering a capability does not grant access to its Project/data. Independent Spaces may leave discovery off. Avoid leaking hidden project names, identifiers, counts or existence through role previews, errors, search, UI, assistant context, events or notification recipients.

## 7. User experience, SDK and operations

- Provide an owner/admin dashboard to define and edit roles, invite members, preview effective access, compare role changes, manage individual exceptions, configure assistant modes, approve sensitive actions, set daily/weekly/monthly credit or action limits and audit changes. Do not silently assign spend mandates; recheck existing billing/credit reservations and provider authorization.
- Offer a member-facing dashboard that shows their actual roles, available resources, assistant capability/limitations, approval requests and the relevant onboarding instructions. Present accurate partial-failure and pending states; support responsive/mobile UI, keyboard/screen-reader access and localization. Avoid exposing private resources by displaying a complete role catalog to unauthorized users.
- Expose versioned SDK and server contracts for role declaration, preview, assignment, permission introspection, action grants and role lifecycle. Resolve scope from host identity; an extension or template cannot self-declare a role into existence as a grant. Use stable opaque IDs, idempotency keys, expected revisions and bounded request/result schemas.
- Record an audit trail for invitation, consent, role assignment/change, exception, effective grant, approval, revocation, access failure and service-principal action. Avoid logging confidential business records, invitation tokens and secrets. Provide bounded metrics and alerts for stuck onboarding, failed reconciliation and unexpected authority changes.

## 9. Acceptance criteria

- A pharmacy template declares Accountant, Inventory, Customer Service and Website Manager. The owner previews and approves an Accountant invitation. After acceptance, accountant and owner access the same explicitly shared accounting records; the accountant and their assistant cannot discover or administer a private website Project or manager-only knowledge. Other businesses and tenants remain isolated.
- Test distinct `inherit`, `read_only` and `none` modes across UI, SDK, knowledge/citations, microapp business actions, files, provider access, channels and delegated workers. A scheduled job without an acting user succeeds only with its own approved service principal; a customer message never gains employee privileges.
- Cover invalid/malicious template declarations, forged and expired invitations, replay, duplicate acceptance, account identity mismatch, policy/version changes during acceptance, partial grant failures, retry/recovery and attempts to give more authority than the inviter possesses. Check member/role/plan limits and correct billing without unwanted provider provisioning.
- Test member suspension, removal and role downgrade during active retrieval, cached answer delivery, assistant action, workflow, channel call and external provider operation. New access is denied, uncertain effects remain explicit, and shared business records and other authorized members remain intact.
- Exercise combined roles, explicit exceptions, sensitive action separation of duties, owner/admin private-project policy, hidden-project non-disclosure, role and template upgrades, version conflicts, backup/restore, export and deletion. Preserve audit evidence without exposing confidential data.
- Verify actual template-builder, owner and member journeys, including mobile/accessibility, on real deployed multi-user services and approved provider integrations with disposable resources. Separate mocked, local-runtime, browser, deployed and provider evidence. Do not claim clinical, legal or production readiness solely from this plan or local tests.
