# Future goal: Naelao helps people create economic value

**Status: Future vision. Do not implement from this document now.** This is a direction to explore after Naelao's core experience is dependable. It is not a product commitment, a guarantee of income, a pricing policy, or an authorization to change billing or build financial features.

## The opportunity

Many people could benefit from capable assistants but face barriers including subscription costs, expensive data, limited connectivity, inaccessible devices, language, time, and limited access to customers or payment systems. The often-repeated claim that 84% of people have never used AI is unverified here; the opportunity does not depend on that exact number.

A future Naelao could help people turn their knowledge, relationships, and goals into useful work that customers choose to pay for. The assistant could find opportunities, prepare proposals, perform approved work, follow up, and maintain records. Where the economics work, revenue could cover the cost of the work and leave a benefit for the user. Earnings are uncertain and depend on genuine demand, fulfillment, costs, and local conditions.

## The person's role

The user remains the owner and decision maker: they define goals and boundaries, bring local context and relationships, decide which opportunities to pursue, and retain control of their accounts, assets, and proceeds. They must be able to inspect and stop work. Naelao and its partners should take responsibility for their own services; the person should not be treated as a liability shield for automated actions.

A future experience should make consequential commitments understandable and obtain the appropriate authorization before acting. It should represent results honestly, protect user data, and provide a way to challenge mistakes, charges, or outcomes. Account ownership, contracts, payments, and applicable legal obligations will require design for each market and workflow.

## A possible value loop

1. The user describes a goal, available resources, and boundaries in a channel and language they can use.
2. The assistant identifies a real opportunity and presents the proposed work, potential customer, expected costs, uncertainties, and required permissions.
3. The user chooses whether to proceed. Naelao carries out approved work using its planner and workers, permitted tools, and relevant extensions.
4. The user and customer can see evidence of what was delivered and whether payment was received.
5. The user sees any agreed provider costs and Naelao fees, and receives their share through an appropriate payment service.

This loop must account for failed work, refunds, disputes, fraud, and provider charges. It must never encourage the assistant to invent customers, misrepresent results, incur uncapped costs, or take unauthorized commitments in pursuit of a commission.

## Business outcomes and commission

For organizations, a future commercial option is to charge a commission on **revenue Naelao helps a business generate**, under a clear agreement with that business. For example, a business might agree to a defined percentage of a paid order that an authorized Naelao workflow helped complete. This is a proposed option, not a current rate or an automatic claim on all business revenue.

Any such agreement needs a measurable outcome, an attribution method, a baseline where appropriate, the time period and rate, treatment of taxes and provider fees, refunds and chargebacks, a fee cap if agreed, audit evidence, and a dispute process. Distinguish revenue from profit and from estimated cost savings. Where attribution is uncertain, use a simpler agreed price or another measurable outcome rather than claiming unverified incremental revenue. The customer should understand the fee before enabling a commission-bearing workflow.

For individuals, explore affordable access and optional outcome-based pricing where fair and practical. Fees should be visible and proportionate, and users should not be charged a success commission on unrelated income. A zero-upfront offer still needs a sustainable funding model for compute, communication, support, and failed attempts.

## Fit with Naelao's architecture

Naelao remains a general AI operating system. Its kernel supplies scoped identity, permissions, execution, approvals, metering, and audit. FDEs and users can create domain-specific tools, extensions, workflows, knowledge, project templates, and Space Templates that use those primitives. Opportunity discovery, customer relationships, business processes, and specialized payment integrations belong in authorized extensions and templates, not a hardcoded business wallet or a universal business-agent persona in the kernel.

Access should be designed for the user's circumstances. Depending on supported channels and local infrastructure, a future workflow might use a web interface, messaging, voice, SMS, or a human intermediary. Feature phones and intermittent connectivity need explicit product research and carrier support; an assistant cannot reach an offline user without a workable communication path.

## How to evaluate the vision later

Start with one repeatable, low-capital service for which identifiable customers already pay. Work with a small group of users and measure paid outcomes, user net earnings after all costs, completion and refund rates, time and human intervention, access barriers, and the quality and reliability of the work. Test the proposed pricing and attribution with real agreements before expanding. Gather evidence from users and customers, and stop or redesign workflows that cannot deliver fair, verifiable value.
